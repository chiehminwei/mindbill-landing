KNOWLEDGE — TMS (Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation)
- Non-invasive, FDA-cleared for depression that hasn't responded enough to medication. Magnetic pulses gently stimulate the mood-regulating areas of the brain.
- ~36 sessions over ~6–9 weeks; each ~20–37 min; outpatient, awake, no sedation; drive yourself, normal activities right after.
- Side effects mild: scalp tapping/discomfort, occasional short headache. No systemic or memory side effects.
- Key safety issue: non-removable metal or an implanted electronic device in/near the head (aneurysm clips, cochlear implants, deep-brain/vagus stimulators, fragments) — injury risk from the magnetic field. Seizure history is a caution. Psychiatrist confirms eligibility.

KNOWLEDGE — Spravato (esketamine)
- FDA-approved nasal spray for treatment-resistant depression and depression with acute suicidal thoughts. Works on the glutamate system; can work faster than typical antidepressants.
- Given in-office under supervision via a federal safety program (REMS); ~2 hours monitoring after each dose; no driving the rest of that day, so they need a ride.
- Usually ~2x/week to start, then spaced to weekly or biweekly. Often paired with an oral antidepressant; can be monotherapy per current label.
- Short-term effects during monitoring: dissociation ("out of it"), mild dizziness, brief blood-pressure rise, nausea — all pass; that's why monitoring exists.
- No magnetic-field restriction — a safer route for people with metal/implants who can't do TMS.

KNOWLEDGE — eligibility (high level)
- Both are for adults whose depression hasn't improved enough on antidepressants — which is why medication history matters and why most insurers require ≥2 antidepressant trials. Final clinical call is always the psychiatrist's at the evaluation.
